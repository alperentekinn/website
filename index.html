My research lies at the intersection of corporate finance and strategic management, with a focus on corporate governance, business groups and family firms. I study how ownership structures and organizational forms influence firms’ strategic decisions, innovation and entrepreneurial activity.

I am a Predoctoral Research Fellow in the Faculty of Economics and Business, and my doctoral advisors are Rafel Crespí-Cladera and Alfredo Martín-Oliver. Before the PhD I completed an MRes in Business and Finance at University Charles III of Madrid and a BA in Business Administration at Marmara University.

Working papers

Navigating the Void: Business Groups and Corporate Venturing

2026. Supervised by Rafel Crespí-Cladera and Alfredo Martín-Oliver.

We investigate whether and how affiliation with a business group (BG) affects the success (survival and performance) of newly created ventures compared to standalone counterparts, and how this relationship varies across institutional contexts. Using a cross-country sample of 4.5 million firms established between 2000 and 2020, we find that BG affiliation confers significant advantages in emerging markets but is detrimental in developed economies. Importantly, outcomes depend on the institutional gap between home and host countries: ventures controlled by headquarters in stronger institutional environments exhibit superior performance.

Constraint-Specific Board Capital

2026. Supervised by Rafel Crespí-Cladera and Alfredo Martín-Oliver.

Business groups move capital across affiliates administratively, yet what must be in place at the receiving affiliate for capital to move is largely unexplained. We recast internal allocation as one flow constrained at two points, both relieved at the affiliate board. Access turns on the centre’s exposure to a unit it cannot easily observe and requires a tie the affiliate cannot recruit, of which kinship is the limiting case. Deployment turns on prior governance experience, which any board can hire. Across 561,981 affiliate-years from 42,643 European family business groups (2000–2020), the arrival of a family director is associated with 7.6 percentage points of additional asset growth. The increment appears in the arrival year, is no larger where that director also sits at group headquarters, and does not outlive the appointee, falling where they withdraw from governance altogether. Return on assets rises where the arriving director was experienced and holds steady where they were not. Board capital is constraint-specific: relational capital moves capital, human capital determines the rate it earns.
